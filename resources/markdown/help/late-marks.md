## What this is

Who was late this month, how often, and by how much — ordered by the figure rather
than by name, and leaving out everybody who was never late.

The Attendance Register already carries late minutes, in a grid thirty-one columns
wide. It answers a different question: *what happened to this person this month*. This
one answers *who do I talk to*.

## Times late and total, side by side

Both columns matter because they are different behaviours. Twelve marks of five
minutes and one mark of an hour are the same hour and not the same conversation: one
is a habit, the other is a morning that went wrong.

**Worst** is the single longest mark, and **Average** is the total over the occasions.
A person whose average is close to their worst was late once; a person whose average
is small and whose total is large is late every day.

## Where "late" comes from

A day is late when the check-in is after the start of the shift the person is on, by
more than the grace the work pattern allows. Change the pattern and the figures change
from then on, not retrospectively — the minutes are recorded on the day.

## Roles and permissions

`ReportView` and the Attendance module. Managers see their own downline, as they do on
every employee-keyed screen.
