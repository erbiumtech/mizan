## What an RFI is for

A question you cannot build without an answer to, asked in writing, with a date by
which the answer is needed.

The register is worth keeping for two reports, and everything on the screen is built
around them: **whose court is it in** — "seventeen RFIs sitting with the Architect" —
and **what is overdue**. A question asked by telephone and answered by telephone is a
question neither side can prove was asked.

## Numbering has no gaps

`RFI-1` upward, per job, assigned for you. You cannot type it and you cannot delete an
RFI.

That is deliberate: the register is read sequentially and quoted by number in
correspondence, so "where is RFI 14?" must never be answerable with "somebody removed
it". A question that no longer needs answering is **cancelled with a reason** and keeps
its number.

## Propose a solution

Fill in **What we propose** wherever you can.

A question with a proposal attached is a yes-or-no. A question without one is homework
for somebody who did not ask for it, and it comes back slower — which shows up in the
response time this register measures.

## Ball in court: a role and a person

Both, and neither is derivable from the other. The **role** is what the register
counts, and it does not change over a two-year job. The **person** is who a chasing
email goes to, and they change three times.

Without the invoicing module there are no contact records, so the role carries the
register on its own. Only the chase loses its address.

## Answer needed by

This is what makes the register a clock rather than a list. An RFI with no date on it
is one nobody is chasing, and the register says so in the column rather than leaving
it blank.

The **Days** column changes meaning with the row: days left while an answer is owed,
days *late* once it is past due, and the response time once it has been answered.

## Impact: flags now, figures later

**Cost** and **time** are `None`, `Possible` or `Yes`, and the estimate beside each one
stays blank until somebody knows.

At raise time nobody knows. Forcing a number produces a column of zeros that four
months later reads as "no impact" when what it meant was "not yet assessed" — and that
is the difference between a register that finds money and one that buries an absence.
`Possible` says somebody looked and cannot yet say, which is a real answer.

## Time impact, no notice <!-- requires: ConstructionDelayUpdate -->

**This is the column to watch.** An RFI marked as having a time impact with no delay
event behind it means a notice period is already running and nothing is chasing it.
Unlike a wrong figure, this failure leaves nothing anywhere for a report to catch — it
is the same silence the delay register exists to prevent, reached from the place the
evidence already sits.

**Raise delay event** on the row starts that clock. It dates the event **the day the
answer was needed**, not today and not the day you asked — the work was not blocked
when you asked, and dating it today is how a claim is time-barred by its own paperwork.

Raising it asks for the delay register's permission rather than this one. Serving
notice on the employer is not the same act as asking a question.

## Recording the answer

Date it **the day the answer was given**, not the day you are typing it. The response
time this register reports belongs to the other side, and dating it from the
transcription flatters them.

A late answer is recorded and marked late. Whether lateness founds a claim is a
contractual question; the row keeps the facts either way.

## Closing needs an answer

**Close** is only offered once there is an answer against the RFI. Closing an
unanswered question would take it off the outstanding list while it is still
outstanding, which is the one job this register has.

Use **Cancel** instead — with the reason — for a question that no longer needs
answering.
