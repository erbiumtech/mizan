## What this screen is

A snapshot of where you stand: what you own, what you owe, and the difference
between them.

It reads only your own records. See the Help on **My Accounts** for who else can
see them.

## How to read it

Three figures, and the third is the one that matters:

- **Assets** — everything you own. Cash, bank balances, anything else you have
  set up as an asset account.
- **Liabilities** — everything you owe. Loans, credit card balances.
- **Net worth** — assets minus liabilities. What would actually be yours if
  every debt were settled today.

Net worth is the number to watch over time. A month where you earned well but
borrowed more can still leave it lower.

## Where the numbers come from

Each account's balance is its **opening balance** plus every transaction you
have recorded against it. Nothing is stored as a running total, so the figures
here can never drift out of step with your transaction list — they are worked
out from it each time you open the page.

That also means the page includes **everything you have recorded**, including
entries dated in the future. If a figure looks too high, check whether you have
post-dated something.

## Why income and expenses are not here

A balance sheet answers "where do I stand?", not "how did the year go?". Your
salary is not something you own — it is money that arrived and then became cash,
or a bank balance, or spending. It has already been counted in the accounts it
flowed into.

For earnings and spending over a period, use **Income & Expenditure**.

## Transfers do not change anything here

Moving money between two of your own accounts leaves your net worth exactly
where it was — one asset goes down, another goes up. If you record a cash
withdrawal as a transfer (which is correct), you will see the two account
balances change and the total stay put.

## Quick answers

**My net worth looks wrong.**
Usually one of three things: an opening balance that was never set on an account
that already had money in it; a transfer recorded as an expense, which makes
money look spent when it only moved; or a post-dated entry already being counted.

**Should I add my house or my car?**
You can — set it up as an asset account with an opening balance. Bear in mind
nothing revalues it for you, so the figure stays whatever you last set until you
change it.

**Why is my credit card a liability rather than a negative asset?**
Because you owe it rather than own it. Keeping it on the liabilities side is
what makes net worth come out right.

**Does the company see this?**
No. These are your own records in your own accounts, and they never reach the
company's books.
