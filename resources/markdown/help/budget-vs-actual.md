What you planned, against what the ledger actually recorded, for one budget over
one period.

## Reading it

Six columns per account:

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| Full year | The whole year's plan for this account |
| Planned | The share of that plan falling inside the dates above |
| Actual | What was posted to the account in those dates |
| Variance | Planned less actual, signed so that **positive is good news** |
| % of year | Actual as a percentage of the full-year plan |

**Variance is signed by whether it is good, not by arithmetic.** Spending less
than planned and earning more than planned are both favourable, and they are
opposite subtractions. A green number means you are in a better position than the
plan on that line, whether it is an income line or an expense line.

## Part months count as part months

Ask on 7 August how a year that began in July is going, and you are measured
against July plus **seven days** of August — not July plus all of August.

This matters more than it sounds. Counting the whole current month is the
ordinary way an overspend is made to look like an underspend, and it does it
silently, every time, until the month ends. The **Full year** column is there so
nothing is hidden: it always shows the untrimmed plan.

## Unbudgeted accounts

An income or expense account with activity in the year but no plan is listed
anyway, marked **unbudgeted**. This is deliberate and it is often the most useful
row on the page — showing only what was planned would hide new spending by
construction, which is the opposite of what a budget review is for.

## Month by month

The lower section breaks the year into months: planned and actual net of income
less expenses, with a bar for each. Months you have not reached yet show their
plan and no actual, so the rest of the year reads as still to come rather than as
a collapse to zero.

## What counts as actual

Only **posted** journal entries. A draft, an entry waiting for approval, and a
rejected one are all invisible here — the same rule the Profit & Loss uses, so
the two reports cannot disagree about the same period.

An entry posted after you looked appears the next time you open the report;
nothing is cached.

## If the figures look wrong

**Everything shows as underspent.** Usually the dates: check the period covers
the months you mean, and that the budget you chose is for the fiscal year those
months are in.

**An expense shows no actual at all.** The plan may be against a group heading
whose children carry the entries. Budget the children.

**Actual is there but planned is zero.** That account is not in the budget — it
should be marked unbudgeted. Add it to the plan if it belongs there.

**Nothing to choose in the Budget list.** Only active budgets are offered.
Create one under Accounting → Budgets, or switch an existing one back on.
