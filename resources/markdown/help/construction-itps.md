## Hold, witness, review — the whole reason an ITP exists

An inspection and test plan says what will be inspected, against what, by whom, and
what happens if they do not come. That last part is the point of the document.

- A **hold point** means work may not proceed past it. Somebody has to attend and
  somebody has to release it.
- A **witness point** means a party is invited and **work may proceed if they do not
  attend**. That is your protection, and it only works if the register shows they were
  told.
- A **review point** is documentation only.

Collapsing those three into a checkbox turns the plan into a formality. Everything on
these two screens follows from taking the distinction seriously.

## A plan is a controlled document

Drafted, then **issued**, then **approved** — and never edited once people are working
to it.

A certification body asks which revision was in force when a given inspection was
carried out, and "the current one" is not an answer. So:

- A draft is editable. An issued plan is not.
- **Revise** copies every point and its parties into a new draft at the next revision
  letter and supersedes the old plan. Inspections carried out last month keep pointing
  at the document that was in force last month.
- **Approving** is a separate permission because the signature is a statement to a third
  party about how the work will be controlled.

**Issuing refuses a hold point that names nobody.** A hold point stops work until
somebody attends, so one with no party is a stoppage waiting for no-one — name the
party, or make it a review point.

## Who attends is a list, not a field

One hold point can need *the Engineer to approve* and *a laboratory to verify*. That is
one point and two parties with two different roles, which is why **Add party** is its
own action.

Mark **attendance mandatory** where the work genuinely cannot proceed without them.
Leaving it off at a witness point is what lets work continue when they do not come.

Set **notice required** in hours. It is a term of this plan for this activity — 24 hours
for a rebar inspection, a week for a third-party load test — and it is copied onto every
inspection raised against the point.

## Requesting an inspection

Raise it from the ITP point wherever there is one. That path **snapshots the point type
and the notice period onto the inspection**, so a later revision of the plan cannot
change what the inspection meant. The same reasoning freezes a certificate's retention
terms and a delay event's notice period.

An inspection against a **draft** plan is refused: it would cite a document nobody has
published, which is the first thing an audit reads for.

**Ad-hoc inspections are fine.** A client's representative walking the site and asking
to see a detail is an inspection, and the register says *ad hoc — no ITP point* rather
than pretending otherwise.

## Record notice separately from the request

**Record notice** is when the attending party was actually told. It is not the same as
the day you raised the paperwork, and a notice period runs from the former.

At a witness point that date is the whole of your protection for proceeding without
them. The register flags **short notice** where the hours given were less than the plan
required, because that is the other side's first answer to "you went ahead without us".

## Recording a result never releases a hold point

**Record result** says what was found. It does not let work proceed.

Leave **the witness attended** off when they were invited and did not come. That is the
fact nobody writes down at the time and the one that decides an argument two months
later — filter the register by *Witness invited and absent* to see them together.

*Passed with comments* counts as accepted: the next operation starts and the comments
become actions. Treating it as a failure would show a job blocked on inspections
everybody involved considers closed.

## Releasing a hold point <!-- requires: ConstructionInspectionRelease -->

**Release hold point** authorises the next operation to start. It is its own permission,
and deliberately not the same act as recording that the inspection passed — on a
certified site those are two people.

It is refused on anything that is not a hold point, on anything not yet inspected, and
on anything that failed. The point exists to stop work until the work is right.

**The badge counts hold points that passed and have not been released.** That is work
standing still for want of a signature nobody knows is missing, and it is the one figure
in this module worth a count on every page.

## The check sheet

Expected against actual, with a unit. "Concrete cube at 28 days" is a number somebody
compares with a standard; a paragraph saying it looked fine is not a record.

**Result has three states.** Blank means nobody has reached that line yet, which is a
different fact from failing it — a sheet that read every unfilled line as a failure
would be worse than no sheet.
