## A permit is time-boxed

**Both ends of the window are datetimes, and both are required.** A permit valid "on the
20th" authorises hot work at four in the morning; a permit valid until 17:00 on the 20th
does not.

**An expired permit that nobody has closed is the failure mode this register exists to
make visible.** It is the count on the sidebar, and nothing in this application closes
one for you — a permit quietly marked closed by a scheduled job is a hazard nobody walked
back to.

## Requesting and issuing are different people

Raising a permit creates a **draft**. Issuing it authorises the work, and that is a
separate permission.

The person who wants to do the work is the last person who should decide it is safe to.
Every permit-to-work regime is built on that separation, and this one is no exception.

**Issuing is refused for a window that has already closed** — authorising work that is
already over is either a mistake or a back-dated cover, and neither should be quiet.

**Issuing is also refused until the controls that type turns on are recorded.** Hot work
needs a fire watch and an extinguisher; a confined space needs a rescue plan and a gas
test; an excavation needs the services scanned. These are the fields the procedure turns
on, and a permit issued without them is a permit nobody thought about.

## The type-specific controls

Everything peculiar to a type goes in **Type-specific controls** as a name and a value:
gas readings, an isolation certificate reference, a rescue plan, wind limits.

The shared fields — where, when, how many people — stay proper fields so the register can
answer *what is open on level four right now*. Thirteen types' fields as columns would be
ninety mostly-empty ones; everything in a bag could not answer that question at all.

## Acceptance

**Record acceptance** when the gang doing the work takes the permit on. A permit issued
and never accepted is a piece of paper rather than an authorisation, and the register
shows the gap — it only exists because both stamps are kept.

## Suspending and resuming

**Suspend** stops the work without ending the authorisation, and needs a reason: whoever
comes back to it has to know what must change before work restarts.

**Resume** is refused once the window has closed. Resuming then would authorise work
outside what somebody signed for — extend it instead.

## An extension is a new permit

**Extend** creates a new permit starting exactly where this one ends, and closes this one.

It never overwrites the end date, because that would destroy the record of what was
authorised *when* — and that is the question a regulator or an insurer asks after
something happens. The extension arrives as a draft, because extending is a request and
issuing is still a decision.

## Closing out <!-- requires: ConstructionPermitIssue -->

**The area made safe is the point of the close-out.** A permit closed with nobody having
walked the area is the sequence that burns a building down an hour after everybody has
gone home.

Closing without it is allowed and needs a sentence saying why — because a permit that
*cannot* be closed is a permit that stays open for ever, and then the expired-and-open
list becomes noise and stops being read. The register keeps a list of permits closed with
nothing recorded, because that is a pattern rather than an event: a site where it is
common is a site where the close-out is a signature.
