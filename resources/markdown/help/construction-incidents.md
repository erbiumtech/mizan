## Report the near misses

**Near miss is the first kind on the list and it is not a lesser one.** Near misses
reported per lost-time injury is the leading indicator that predicts the next injury —
which means a site reporting many of them is a site where people speak up, and a site
reporting none is not a safe site, it is a quiet one.

Reporting is the widest permission in this module for exactly that reason. Anybody who
can see something nearly go wrong should be able to write it down.

Unsafe acts, unsafe conditions and property damage belong here too. So does everything
from first aid up to a fatality. **One register**, because an organisation that keeps
near misses somewhere else cannot compute the ratio that predicts its next injury.

## The time, not the date

**When it happened is a datetime and it is required.** Shift timing is half the
analysis: hour ten of a twelve-hour shift, the first hour back from a break, the last
night of a run of nights. None of that survives a date column.

## The reporting delay is itself a measure

**When it was reported is a separate field** and the form shows you the gap while you
type.

A site that takes four days to report a first-aid case is a site where the next one is
not reported at all. That is why the delay is on the face of the register, and why the
policy threshold — 24 hours unless your company sets another — is configuration rather
than a judgement built into the software.

## The injured person's name works on its own

Type it in. Most people on most sites are a subcontractor's employees and are in no
table here, and pretending otherwise loses the record entirely.

**Time lost is a separate field from days lost.** A lost-time injury where nobody yet
knows how long somebody will be off is the ordinary state for a fortnight — deriving the
flag from a day count would classify it as a medical-treatment case for exactly as long
as the reportable clock is running.

## Witnesses, and when you spoke to them

A name is enough. The date the statement was taken is the column that matters: one taken
on the day is worth several taken three weeks later, and the table shows the gap so
nobody has to work it out.

An investigation that cannot say when it spoke to somebody is an investigation nobody
can weigh.

## Reportable to an authority <!-- requires: ConstructionIncidentInvestigate -->

Mark it as soon as it has been assessed. **Reportable with nothing recording that
anybody told them is the one exposure in this register with a statutory clock on it**,
and it is the only thing here counted on the sidebar — because it is the only one that
cannot be recovered by looking later.

**Report to authority** records who was told, their reference and the date. A regulator
asks for the date.

## Closing needs a cause <!-- requires: ConstructionIncidentInvestigate -->

**Close** is refused without an immediate or root cause recorded. An incident closed with
no cause is a lesson nobody learned, and a register where everything is closed and
nothing is understood is worse than one showing the truth.

It is also refused while a reportable incident has no authority date — closing one would
file a statutory duty as finished.

**Reopen** exists, with a reason, because new facts do come out.
