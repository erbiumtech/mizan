## What this shows

Every receipt where a customer paid an invoice short and handed over a tax
deduction certificate for the rest — the customer's side of section 153. One row
per certificate: the date the money arrived, the customer, the invoice, the
certificate reference, and the amount withheld.

The total is what sits in **1260 Advance Income Tax**, and it is the figure to
claim against the company's own income tax return.

## Where the rows come from

When a receipt is recorded with "Tax withheld by the customer" filled in, the
receipt's journal entry debits the bank for what arrived and 1260 for what the
customer deducted, and the invoice is settled by the two together. This report
reads those 1260 debits straight from the ledger, so its total always agrees with
the trial balance — a list built from anything else could not promise that.

The certificate reference is whatever was typed on the receipt. A row that says
"no certificate" is a receipt somebody recorded without one; the invoice's history
shows who, and the certificate is worth chasing before the return is filed.

## The period

Fiscal year to the date chosen, like the other statutory reports. Withholding is
claimed per tax year, so that is the span the return needs.

## What is not here

- **What this company withholds when it pays suppliers.** That is the other side
  of section 153, deducted on the payment at approval, and reported on the
  Withholding Statement.
- **Receipts in a foreign currency.** A certificate is a rupee document about a
  rupee liability, so tax withheld can only be recorded on an invoice billed in the
  base currency.
