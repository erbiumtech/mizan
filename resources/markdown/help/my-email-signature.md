## What this is

Your email signature, built from your employee record and the company letterhead.

The point is not that it saves typing. It is that forty people's signatures then say
the same company name, spelt the same way, with the same address and the same website
— and when the company moves, they all change at once.

## Using it

**Outlook**: File → Options → Mail → Signatures, then paste the markup from the second
box.

**Gmail**: Settings → General → Signature, then paste the **preview** at the top of
this page rather than the markup. Gmail takes the formatted version and shows raw tags
if you give it HTML.

**Apple Mail**: Settings → Signatures, and paste the preview.

The third box is the same thing as plain text, for a client that refuses HTML and for
the bottom of a reply.

## Why it is a table with inline styles

Not a style choice. Mail clients strip stylesheets and most still lay out with tables;
a signature built the way a web page is built arrives as a column of unstyled text in
Outlook, which is where most of these end up.

## Why there is no logo

A logo in a signature is an attachment on every mail you send, it is blocked by
default in half the clients that receive it, and it leaves a grey box with a red cross
where the brand was meant to be. The colour band does the same job in nine bytes.

## Changing what it says

Change your record, or ask HR to. The phone and the title come from your employee
record; the company name, address and website come from Company Settings → Letterhead;
the colours and the typeface from the brand kit beside them.

## Roles and permissions

No permission. Everybody with an employee record sees their own and nobody else's.
