## What this screen is

Everything you have recorded in your own books, newest first — what came in,
what went out, and what you moved between your own accounts.

Like everything in this module, it is private to you. See the Help on **My
Accounts** for exactly who can and cannot see it.

## How an entry actually works

Every transaction here is recorded twice, once on each side. Buying PKR 12,000
of school books is not one fact but two: your **Education** spending went up by
12,000, *and* your **Bank account** went down by 12,000. Recording both is what
makes the totals trustworthy — your balance sheet cannot drift away from your
spending, because they are two views of the same entries.

You never have to do that arithmetic. The three buttons below each build both
sides for you. It matters only because it explains why an entry cannot be
half-edited later.

## Recording income <!-- requires: PersonalFinanceCreate -->

Click **Record income**. You fill in:

- **Date**
- **Amount**
- **What kind of income** — one of your income categories. How this money is
  taxed comes from the category you pick, not from anything you choose here.
- **Paid into** — which of your accounts received it.
- **Description**

## Recording an expense <!-- requires: PersonalFinanceCreate -->

Click **Record expense**. Same shape:

- **Date**
- **Amount**
- **What it was for** — one of your expense categories, such as Education.
- **Paid from** — which account the money came out of.
- **Description**

## Moving money between your own accounts <!-- requires: PersonalFinanceCreate -->

Click **Transfer** — for a cash withdrawal, or moving money from one account to
another. Your **net worth does not change**: the same money is simply somewhere
else, so a transfer never appears as income or as spending. This is worth using
properly rather than recording a withdrawal as an expense, which would make it
look like the money was gone.

You cannot transfer an account to itself.

## Why there is no "New" button

Deliberate. A generic form would ask you to enter two balanced lines by hand to
record a bus fare, and you would stop using it by Wednesday. The three actions
above cover what people actually do, and each one produces a correct pair.

## Why an entry cannot be edited <!-- requires: PersonalFinanceDelete -->

There is no Edit. To fix a mistake, **delete the entry and record it again**.

An entry is two linked halves that have to stay equal. An edit form that let you
change one side without the other would produce books that do not add up, and
one that carefully rewrote both is more machinery than a personal ledger
justifies. Deleting and re-recording takes about the same number of clicks and
cannot leave anything inconsistent.

## What the columns mean

- **Category** — the income or expense account the entry hit. A transfer between
  two of your own accounts touches neither, so it shows as **Transfer**.
- **Amount** — the value of the entry.
- **Movement** — where the money came from and went to, as
  `Bank account → Education`. Hidden by default; turn it on from the column
  menu if you want to see it.

## Filtering

- **Tax year** — the July–June year the entry falls into, which is set from the
  entry's own date.
- **From / Until** — any date range you like.

## Quick answers

**I recorded an expense but my balance sheet did not change.**
Check what you paid *from*. If the entry credited an account you rarely use, the
money left that one. Turn on the **Movement** column to see both sides.

**I withdrew cash from the bank — income or expense?**
Neither. That is a **Transfer** from your bank account to your cash account. You
are no poorer, so it should not count as spending.

**I picked the wrong category.**
Delete the entry and record it again. There is no edit — see above.

**Can I record something dated last year?**
Yes. Set the date you want and it books into whichever tax year contains that
date, not the one you happen to be in now.

**Why does my expense category show a positive balance?**
Expense accounts grow as you spend against them. A large Education balance means
you have spent a lot on education, not that you are owed anything.
