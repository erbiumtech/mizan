## What this shows

For every project with hours or money against it in the period: the hours booked,
what those hours cost in salary, what was billed to the project by suppliers, what
was invoiced for it, and the margin left — in money and as a percentage of revenue.

This is the report that says whether a client is worth having. A fixed-price project
going underwater shows here while there is still time to rescope it.

## Where the figures come from

- **Hours** — every timesheet entry dated in the period, billable or not, approved or
  not. Cost is incurred whether or not anybody approved the hour.
- **Labour** — each hour priced at what that person's payslip paid for an hour in
  that month: the month's total earnings over the hours their work pattern says they
  were contracted for (or the payslip's working days at eight hours, for a company
  without Attendance). An allowance costs the hour as much as the basic does, so
  earnings rather than basic wage.
- **Other cost** — supplier bills issued in the period and tagged with the project,
  net of tax, less any debit notes.
- **Revenue** — sales invoices issued in the period and tagged with the project, net
  of tax, less credit notes. In the company's base currency, as the ledger has them.

## Uncosted hours

An hour with no payslip behind it — a contractor, a month payroll has not run yet, a
company without the payroll module — is counted in *Hours* and shown in *Uncosted*,
and is **not** in the labour figure. The note under the totals says how many. A
margin that silently priced those hours at zero would look complete and be wrong in
the direction that flatters.

## The period

Fiscal year to the date chosen, like the financial statements. Margin is a period
figure, and the year is the period a company judges a client over. Pick an earlier
date to read the year as it stood then.

## What is not in it

Employer statutory contributions (EOBI, social security) are a cost of the hour too,
but they are posted from a different figure and are not in *Labour* yet. Overheads
that are nobody's project — rent, the office internet — are not here either; this is
a contribution margin, not a full profit and loss by project. *Internal* projects
appear with cost and no revenue, which is what internal work is.
